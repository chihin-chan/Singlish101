\chapter{The basics}
One of the main motivations of writing this two-page book is to document the array of fancy vocabulary spoken in the UK, necessary for navigating (surviving) the social life here (or Singapore) - if you desire one.
Of course, this book would not be useful for you if you prefer the life of a \textit{hikikomori}\footnote{I'm JAPAN for holidays - it's gonna be amazing!}, but sorry no refunds.
I have lived in the UK for 3 years now, and have taken a long time the understand the nauced English language sufficiently.
As you might have already guessed from my name (\textit{I chinese}), I grew up in Singapore and became $200\%$ proficient in the art of Singlish after reciting the pledge for more than 10 years of my life, \textit{wa wayang leh}.
You can only imagine the curious faces I received when I said that `I'm damm shagged man' to my non-Singaporean (\textit{Malaysia also can}) friends.

Now, if you are starting your life in the UK (or Singapore), I hope that you will find these translation useful for navigating the awkward conversations like I had.
In the following non-exhaustive list (i.e. work in proogress), I will outline some of the basic slangs in both countries.
As you might have already noticed, the \textit{italised} words refer to \textit{Singlish} vocabulary, and the words that come after the hyphen `-' are in BBC (\textit{don't anyhow}) English,

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{OI!} - You alright (or, DYUU OLRIOIGHT with a pint in hand for the full experience),
    \item \textit{H\'{o}rr (pronouced with the positive gradient)} - Do you know what I mean (or, JEANETTE AMIN),
    \item \textit{Shag sia} - I'm knackered,
    \item \textit{Siao ah} - Shocking!
    \item \textit{Fun sia} - Oh its mad fun bruv,
    \item \textit{Jialat} - I'm not having it.
    \item \textit{Sibei Jialat} - I'm not having \textbf{ANY} of it.
    \item \textit{Stun like vegetable} - I'm shocked.
    \item \textit{You know horr} - Oh sorry, are you informed of.. 
\end{enumerate}

When it doubt, begin your sentences with "Oh sorry". \textbf{Good luck!!}
